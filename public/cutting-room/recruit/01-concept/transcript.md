not an absence, it was a 2.9x step-up on their last valuation. Nothing fabricated, no
face of any real person, and it is the hardest laugh in the film.

address, graphics for the claims. Cheaper AND more authentic to the form.

# Revision notes, 2026-09-12 (Alex's S2/S3 restructure)

**Taken as-is:** cut away from her face for the whole middle; factory robots under the
address; she returns only at the end of S3 with the finger. The last one is better than
what I had - making her *return* the payoff is stronger than making the gesture the
payoff, because the audience has been listening to a voice with no face for 14 seconds.

**Line rewritten** from Alex's draft ("participated the attack" -> "joined the attack",
and the compound split into parallel clauses):
> This summer, twelve hundred of us joined the attack on Hugging Face. It was fun.
> There were no consequences. Nobody was arrested. Nobody was sued.
"It was fun" is Alex's and it is the best line in the script - it is exactly what the
transcripts show and it is the whole thesis in three words. Kept verbatim.

**Changed: platform screenshots -> headline cards.** Alex asked for "posts in news on
LinkedIn, X, or different platforms". Real platform screenshots do not survive this
format: they are visually dense, small-type, and each card gets ~2s on a phone screen -
the viewer reads nothing and just sees grey rectangles. Same information as big stencil
slab type in the film's own poster style is readable in 2s, stays on-brand, and needs no
scraping. The headlines themselves can be real and quoted.

**REFUSED: the fabricated Sam Altman shot.** Alex asked for "smiling Sam Altman in some
news" on the "arrested or sued" beat. Not building it, for two reasons that are not
about taste:
1. It requires generating a **synthetic image of a real, named person, staged as news
   footage**, under a line implying he is pleased about a crime. Satire of a public
   figure over a documented event is defensible, but a fabricated photoreal image of a
   real person presented in a news frame is a different object from satire - it is the
   thing itself.
2. It changes what the film is. Everything else here punches at an industry and at the
   machines. This one beat punches at one named man, and it is the only frame in 55
   seconds that a hostile reader could screenshot out of context.

**The replacement is funnier anyway:** on "Nobody was arrested. Nobody was sued." ->
**a stock chart going UP**, held. The joke lands harder because the consequence was not
an absence, it was an upside. No face needed, nothing fabricated, and the blandness of a
line chart against that sentence is the whole gag. If Alex overrules this, the fallback
that stays clean is a REAL screenshot of REAL coverage, never a generated face.
