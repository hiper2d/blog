Act 1 · casting

One android, nine costumes

The lead is K-07, the caretaker of a deep-space ship, the only one awake. She started as a tired woman in a jumpsuit. Alex wanted her "younger and more gothy", then "more robot, plastic-leather costume, she never changes it - a robot-doll." She ended up changing it for every section of the song.

Casting · first favourite

The goth face. Black hair with uneven micro bangs, smudged eyeliner, plum lips, a seam on her cheek and a small amber light at her temple. That light became the one warm colour in the whole film.

The robot-doll

The model engraved "K-07" on her name plate without being asked. It became her name.

Kept · the canon

A character sheet: front, profile, full body. Every later image starts from this face as a reference.

Intent beats measurements

For the glossy suit Alex said the hips were too wide. The next prompt listed body specs: narrow straight hips, a slim waist, a flat chest, long thin arms, a small head. The result was a skeletal mannequin. Alex called it "terrible" and gave a better instruction: "don't give exact guides, just make her look good, a perfect-body android". One line of intent landed on the first try.

Kept · "better"

"Make her look good" stayed in every prompt after this. Precise specs still work for outfits, props and staging. They backfire for bodies. (The mannequin was deleted, so there is no picture.)

A new look per section

The rule from Alex: every new section gets a new look. The face stays the same, the place and the clothes change.

Nine looks, one face

In film order. The captain coat is the only one that covers more than one section. The gloss suit opens the film and comes back for "Still here" at the end.

Final · chorus 3 flips through the old looks at half a second each, then back to the cryo bay

Act 2 · the beat

The kick is late

Every cut, step and tap is meant to land on the kick drum. The first beat grid came from librosa: 120 BPM, a beat every 0.500 s from 9.68 s. It looked right and it was not. The real kick was always a little earlier than the grid, and the gap grew over the song.

Grid saidKick measuredEarly by
16.180 s16.136 s0.044 s, about a frame
30.180 s30.110 s0.070 s
50.680 s50.588 s0.092 s, more than two frames

The fix: kick onsets measured on a 120 Hz low-passed envelope, then a straight line fitted through them: kick k = 9.576 + 0.50022 × k seconds. Every cut in the film sits on that line. A second bug hid behind it: the renderer picked clip frames with a float that rounded down, so about every third frame was shown twice and the next one skipped. Smooth motion looked off-beat until both were fixed.

A finger that taps on the beat

A bored finger tapping on a table, a callback to "nothing happens". The Seedance clip tapped at its own speed, so each tap was found in the pixels and retimed onto the kicks. Then came rounds of notes on rhythm and lift: one tap per two beats, then back to every beat with a smaller lift, and then "the finger never touches the table". The loop had started on the frame where the lift already began. Moving it two frames back fixed it.

Kept · "This is perfect"

The almost-human hand won over the black robot hand. One tiny steel ring at the base of the finger.

Raw · the model's own tempo, two taps a second · $1.17
Final · a small lift, contact on every kick, rebuilt from four frames of the real clip

Steps on the beat

The opening walk is only her legs. Optical flow could not find the steps because the camera moved too much, so the heel strikes were marked by eye on frame sheets and each one was pinned to a beat. Alex's note on the clip itself: "not what I expected - why is she turning around". The camera swings behind her in the first seconds, so the edit uses the straight part as much as it can.

Raw · the camera drifts from her side to behind her · $2.36
Final · drums come in, heels land on the beat

The boot stomp

In the instrumental build a combat boot under the red dress stamps on the stage. It took five tries.

Rules that came out of this: cut on measured kick attacks, not on the tracker's grid. Check which beats carry the strong kick in each section. A picture that lands a frame or two early reads as on time; one that lands late never does.

Act 3 · the mouth

Lip sync by ear, not by Whisper

Seedance can animate a face from a still and a slice of the vocal. It sings the line, but it does not always keep the timing. It may start late, or stretch a line. The plan was to measure its delay with Whisper word times and shift the clip. That worked on the first test and then failed badly.

Test 1, raw · with the model's own audio; line 2 comes 0.44 s early · $2.36
Fixed · the closed-mouth gap stretched by 1.31x, the real song under it. Alex: "good job"

Verse 5: off by 1.4 seconds

In the sweater verse Alex said "Maybe they're busy" was not lip-synced. A flat 0.4 s shift had fixed line one, but the model sang line two almost twice as slow. That got remapped word by word from Whisper times. Alex: "v5 line 2 still misplaced". Whisper itself was the problem. On Seedance audio, with its own room noise under the voice, its word times were wrong by about a second.

Start frame · verse 5

Listening for Earth. The clip was made from this still and an 8 s slice of the vocal.

Raw, model audio · line 2 sung 1.4 s later than the song · $2.34
Final · both lines remapped from the envelope

What worked: band-pass both tracks to 250-3500 Hz, where the voice lives and the ambience drops out, take the loudness envelope, and read where each line starts and ends. Line 2 was 4.83-5.85 s in the song and 6.25-7.20 s in the clip: the same length, 1.4 s late. A few anchor points remap the clip onto the song.

Act 4 · the camera

Stills that move

Most of the 157 shots are stills, not clips. A still that sits there for a second is dead, so each one gets a camera move. The simple version is a push into a 4K image. Even that had two bugs, and Alex saw both.

The plate · 3840 x 2160

Chorus 2 opened on a zoom out from her face. Later a clip replaced it.

Bug 1 · a smooth ease: "the zoom-out was fast then stopped"
Bug 2 · "the nozzle zoom-out wobbles": the picture slides sideways
Fixed · one point of the image stays put while the frame widens

Bug 1: a zoom has to change the frame width geometrically, by the same ratio each frame, or the eye reads it as slowing down. A new ease called "zoom" does that, at constant speed with no ease in or out. Bug 2 had two causes. First, the crop window was clamped inside the image, so a zoom centred near an edge got pushed sideways as it widened. Then, with the new ease, the width moved geometrically but the centre still followed the old curve. Now the centre moves with the width, so one image point stays fixed on screen.

Native close-ups, never crops

The bridge is a wide shot of her on a stage in a red dress. Alex asked if it was a draft. It was not: every clip is 1280 x 720, and in that wide shot her face was about 130 pixels across. His rule: "zoomed images go low-res, so a native close-up image, never a crop". So a new still was made as a close-up, and a new clip from it.

Too small for a face

Great as a picture, weak as a singing shot. It stayed in the cut for the first and last line.

Kept · made as a close-up

The face fills the frame, so 720p holds up. This is also the cover of this page.

Final · "But where is around?" · $2.34

From a push to 2.5D

Then the effects layer. Alex: "first of all animate the stills. The animated parts can be touched but lightly." Each still got a depth map made locally, so the camera can orbit a little and the foreground moves against the background. Lamps and screens flicker, dust floats in the light. Clips only get the colour grade and a lyric line.

Left · the first cut, flat pushes · right · the final intro, stills in 2.5D, labels and grade

Moves stay small: an orbit of about 3 percent and a dolly of up to 10 percent. Anything bigger stretches at the depth edges. The depth maps cost nothing; they ran on the laptop CPU.

Act 5 · the screens

Screens drawn in code

The ship is full of old screens, and the song is about reports nobody reads. Image models cannot be trusted with exact text or digits, so every screen was made blank on purpose and its content drawn in code, timed to the song. The text is typed on the measured sung words, bars follow the real spectrum, lights flash on the kicks.

A pulsar that keeps time

A pulsar spins once per beat. The image model painted the beams in, fixed, so they were edited out and drawn in code: two cones from the poles, and a flash when a beam sweeps past the lens. The first version flashed four times a second, all the same. Alex: "It doesn't pulse in rhythm". The flashes were on time; with no accent the eye lost the kick. Now the main pulse snaps on with the kick and the second beam is a weak interpulse, like a real pulsar.

Plate · beams removed

Only the hot spots kept. The beams come from code.

Plate · the heart is real

The model drew the grease-pencil heart. The sweep, the blips and their labels are code. The sweep crosses Gerald's blip on the word "Gerald".

Act 8 · the finish

Upscale and numbers

Alex said the picture felt below 1080p. True: every clip comes out of Seedance at 1280 x 720. Running Real-ESRGAN on the laptop took about 20 seconds a frame, around 20 hours for the whole film, and it waxed the skin. Two cloud upscalers were tested on the bridge clip. Alex picked SeedVR2: "It's good actually".

Same frame, same region, 1:1 pixels

Eyes, hair strands, the mic grille: sharper. Mouth shapes stayed identical frame for frame, so the lip sync work survived. All 32 clips in the cut, about 35 minutes with four running at once.

Left · 720p scaled · right · SeedVR2, same crop, in motion
Cloud test · $1.65

Stretch, Topaz, SeedVR2. Both cloud tools were close; SeedVR2 was cheaper.

Local test · too slow

Free, but 20 hours, and the grain was gone.

Rendering only what changed

The whole edit is a JSON file of 157 shots, rendered frame by frame in a headless browser. A full render took about 25 minutes, and Alex's notes came faster than that. Alex: do not re-render everything each time. The renderer now cuts the song into 8 s chunks, keeps each finished chunk by a fingerprint of its shots, and renders four at a time. A cold build took 8 minutes; a build with no changes renders nothing and only joins the chunks.
