Act 1 · the run

Her hips, three ways

Episode four opens where episode three ended: after the "Boo", she runs. The start frame already existed, the running plate from the ep3 look tests. The first clip moved beautifully and had one problem Alex spotted at once: the low camera behind her pushed her hips wider than any reference.

Reference · from episode three

Never animated in episode three. A motion reference that sat unused for a week and became the first second of the next film.

Rejected · both

Slimmer, then a higher camera. The slim edit went too far ("now hips are too narrow") and the raised camera read like surveillance but lost the drama. "I liked the old one better."

Kept · the middle one

About ten percent, original camera. The edit asked for a subtle change and attached a body reference with the right proportions. The animation prompt said "natural athletic build", not "narrow hips".

Killed · great motion, the model widened her as she ran · $1.48
Kept · slim through the whole run; two pods flare orange as she passes · $1.48

Out of a door, not down another hall

A second run toward the camera in the mushroom corridor was planned as a straight line. Alex changed it: she bursts out of a side door, almost falls, looks both ways, then runs at us. The lamps above her light up one at a time as she passes, the first sign that the ship is following her.

Kept

The door landed closer than asked. Better: her face is bigger. Near lamps dark, far lamps lit, so the animation only had to switch them on.

Kept, trimmed at 5.0s · every beat in order; the last second is her chest filling the lens, cut off · $1.77

Act 2 · the canteen

She hides, then the light comes on

The old crew canteen from episode two, a tree grown through the long table. Alex's staging: she runs in, hides under the table, and only then does the lamp above her switch on. Then he added a camera flying around the table while she dives under it.

Rejected · wrong medium

Perfect staging, the source plate's look. The ep2 room was rendered sepia, and the edit followed it instead of our cel register. No green anywhere.

Last frame · lamp on

Restyled, composition untouched. Flat cel, green mushrooms back, the lamp the only orange.

First frame · lamp off

A different angle on purpose. High and wide from the entrance, so the model has to fly the camera down and around to reach the last frame.

Kept, first try · she runs in, the camera swoops around the table, she curls up, and at 7.0s the lamp snaps on · $2.36

Measured, not assumed: the fear was that a lit last frame would make the model fade the light up across the whole clip. Brightness per frame said otherwise: on, off, on, off, on, in five frames, then it holds. Then a new trap: converting to twos (every frame held twice, the hand-drawn look) would have eaten that flicker, so the last second of this clip runs at full frame rate.

Act 3 · the goddess

A camera that pulls back from an eye

After the lamp, cut to the one who switched it on. Alex asked for a zoom out, and then for a real camera move instead of a digital zoom on a still. To stop the model inventing her body, both ends were pinned: a new close-up of the lens as the first frame, the canonical episode two plate as the last.

First frame

One lens, no face. The series rule held. The red light in the centre is her colour.

Last frame · episode two canon

No red in this lens. So the model faded the light out on the way back. Accepted.

Rejected · the same clip on twos: "feels like low fps"
Kept · native 24 fps, 136 of 145 frames unique · $1.77

Rule written into the series bible: twos are for figure motion only. A camera move on twos judders, because the whole frame steps at twelve frames a second.

Act 4 · eyes everywhere

She has no magic eyes. She has cameras.

The plan for the second half was to calm down. Alex refused: "there is no reason to calm yet. How can the Goddess see her? She can't, only through the cameras." So the voice explains itself with a slideshow of cameras, and the last one zooms into a lens.

Slide 1 · slides sideways

Brutal, as asked: concrete, metal, unknown machines. "A little too much of cameras" was the note. At a second on screen, density reads as "everywhere".

Slide 2 · tilts down

The Evangelion shot. Rings of eyes, built to watch.

Slide 3 · zooms into the lens

Cut down to three cameras after the note. They grew into the bark like knots. The lowest one is the camera she will blind.

Too fast · 0.8 / 0.8 / 1.6s · $0
Kept · 1.2 / 1.2 / 2.2s, the zoom made with zoompan on a 4x upscale to kill the jitter · $0

From fear to decision

Alex asked for one more beat: a close frame of her under the table, hand over her mouth, and then something changes. She lowers the hand, breathes, and stares into the lens for a few seconds. In the cut it answers the zoom: the camera looks at her, she looks back.

Kept

The strongest face frame of the episode. "No 3D render look" said twice in the prompt.

Kept · the hand drops at 2.5s, the stare holds from 4s. The model's own audio came back nearly empty · $1.77

Act 5 · the extinguisher

Blind them one at a time

The first plan was a smash. Alex had a better one: a fire extinguisher, foam on the lenses, and one clip from the camera's point of view turned into three feeds with different crops and offset timing, so they do not show the same moment. Then he split the clip in two, because crawling out and spraying is too much for one render.

Start of clip one

The extinguisher planted in the plate. So the grab has somewhere to go.

End of clip one · start of clip two

One frame shared by two renders. The join between them is invisible by construction.

Half kept · the table shrinks at 3.6-4.9s; the good ends became two feeds. First submit blocked by moderation, the identical resubmit passed · $1.77
Kept · no hose, she aims the cylinder like a cannon; the foam runs down the lens · $1.77
Superseded · two feeds froze into static · $0
Kept · "switch to the second one, so all of them end up sprayed out" · $0

Monochrome, scanlines, the red REC dot and the labels are all post. The clips were rendered in colour so her face stays on model; a grey start frame would have drifted her.

Not her

The face was fixed and then rejected anyway. "Right now, none of the images we have are her." The image model's close-up face had drifted from the girl in the clips.

Reference · from our own clips

Her face, pulled from the video we already had. Alex picked two: the steady stare and the determined look up at the camera.

Kept · the end frame

The impact itself as the last frame. Left hand under, right hand pushing, cold and in control. The model then had to animate a real hit.

She raised her arms too high. Who smashes cameras like that? If I needed to punch something hard with a fire extinguisher, I'd use a thrust. Left arm holds it from below, the right one does the pushing. She calmed down and got the situation under control.Alex, directing the third take

Act 7 · the ending

A melting lamp and a railing she walked through

She walks out of the canteen and onto a catwalk above the ship's spine, a hollow megastructure with forest on its bridges and a tower far away. Two clips, two defects, and one of them taught the most useful rule of the episode.

Kept · camera on the table

Alex's angle. Foam and mushrooms huge in the foreground, her walking away small.

Defect · the bulb bulges and drips like it is melting · $1.48
Kept, fixed for free · the lamp from frame 0 pasted over the clip; three patch shapes until the doorway stayed live · $0
The cause

A good image that disagreed with the start frame. Made on its own, then used as the last frame for an empty-catwalk first frame. The railing did not match.

The fix · built from the start frame

An edit of the empty catwalk that only adds her. The railing is the same pixels in both frames; checked along both rails before paying for the clip.

Killed · at 3.4-3.8s she walks through the near railing · $2.07
Superseded · the rails painted back over her, three masks, "still glitchy" · $0
Kept · she walks to the end, stops, looks up, impressed; the railing stays in front of her · $2.07

Rule for first+last renders: if the two frames disagree about the geometry, the model will find a path through it. Build the end frame as an edit of the start frame, place the character where nothing has to cross her, and check the overlapping parts before the render. A patch can hide the crossing but not remove it.

Act 9 · sound and subtitles

Twenty-two stems and a yellow machine

Most of the film was silent until the end. The sound bed is procedural, twenty-two stems from one script: the ship's hum, cicadas and drips, lamp clicks and mains buzz, the iris servo, camera relays, the spray, the crack of the lens, a sub hit on the black, wet steps and steps on metal grating. Then subtitles, because most people meet a short with the sound off.

Style board · $0

"I like yellow." The speaker voice became amber Courier capitals in brackets, over a soft dark band so it survives the pale feeds. Her close voice stayed thin white with a faint red glow.
